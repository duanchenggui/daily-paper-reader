## 摘要
生成式人工智能（GenAI）正在使软件工程学生的工作从产出制品转向评估和监督AI生成的输出。然而，对于学生在报告干预时会在输出中识别出哪些特征，以及他们在真实软件工程工作中如何行使监督，目前知之甚少。我们分析了145名软件工程一年级学生通过结构化AI日志记录的962个学生—AI交互轮次，其中包括172次学生声明的干预。采用多维编码框架，我们刻画了这些报告干预中识别出的输出特征、学生进行干预的原因、他们采取的行动以及这些行动的实施方式。在74.4%的干预中识别出适配性问题，而涉及正确性问题的干预占16.9%，这表明监督更常关注看似合理的AI输出是否适合工程任务，而非其是否包含显式错误。在可分类的干预中，学生最常修改生成的输出（51.0%）或细化提示词（28.2%），这两类回应通常都与工程适配性相关。学生内部分析进一步表明，与被认为完美的输出相比，被认为包含轻微缺陷的输出与更低的评估信心相关。综上所述，这些发现将新手AI监督刻画为一个超越错误检测的过程，既包括对目的适配性的判断，也包括在AI辅助工程工作中的主动干预。

## Abstract
Generative AI (GenAI) is shifting software engineering students' work from producing artefacts towards evaluating and supervising AI-generated outputs. However, little is known about what characteristics students identify in outputs when reporting interventions, or how they exercise oversight during authentic software engineering work. We analysed 962 student-AI interaction turns documented by 145 first-year software engineering students through structured AI Journals, including 172 student-declared interventions. Using a multi-dimensional coding framework, we characterised the output characteristics identified in these reported interventions, students' reasons for intervening, the actions they took, and how those actions were enacted. Fitness problems were identified in 74.4% of interventions, compared with 16.9% involving correctness problems, indicating that oversight more often concerned whether plausible AI outputs were suitable for the engineering task than whether they contained explicit errors. Among classifiable interventions, students most frequently modified generated outputs (51.0%) or refined their prompts (28.2%), with both response types commonly associated with engineering fit. Within-student analysis further showed that outputs perceived as containing minor flaws were associated with lower evaluation confidence than outputs perceived as perfect. Taken together, these findings characterise novice AI oversight as a process that extends beyond error detection to encompass both judgement of fitness for purpose and active intervention in AI-assisted engineering work.
