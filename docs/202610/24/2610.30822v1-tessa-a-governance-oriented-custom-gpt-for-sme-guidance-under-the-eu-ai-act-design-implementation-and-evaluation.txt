# Abstract 

The increasing adoption of generative AI across industries created opportu-nities for innovation while introducing growing AI governance and regulatory challenges for organization. Small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) must navigate compliance requirements under the European Union Artifi-cial Intelligence Act (EU AI Act). Although large language models (LLMs) offer regulatory and educational support, concerns remain regarding gover-nance alignment, reliability, and the practical applicability. To address this challenge, TESSA, a governance-oriented educational artifact, was devel-oped within the OpenAI Custom GPT environment. Using a design science research approach, TESSA was evaluated through a multidimensional frame-work consisting of governance alignment assessment, human expert review, and consistency testing across 100 benchmark questions in ten thematic categories. The evaluation compared TESSA with a standard ChatGPT baseline using identical benchmark questions and assessment procedures. preliminary LLM-assisted governance alignment reached 90% for TESSA and 83% for the baseline, while independent validation by an AI regulation lawyer produced comparable rates of 71% and 70%, respectively. Human expert review indicated that both systems provided generally strong gov-ernance guidance. Consistency testing showed highly stable performance across repeated executions, with average overall consistency score of 0.969 for TESSA and 0.976 for the baseline. The findings suggest that governance-oriented grounding provides structured and practical compliance guidance while maintaining educational quality and response stability. The study con-tributes (i) a governance-oriented Custom GPT artifact for AI governance education for SMEs, (ii) a human-validated regulatory update mechanism, 1and (iii) a multidimensional evaluation framework that can support the eval-uation of governance-oriented AI artifact.

# 1 Introduction 

The rapid adoption of generative AI has accelerated the integration of AI into products, services, and business processes across software businesses and other industries. Consequently, understanding and complying with AI regulations, particularly the EU AI Act, has become an important challenge. This is especially true for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), which often lack dedicated legal expertise and governance resources while pursu-ing AI-driven innovation. The EU AI Act introduces a risk-based regulatory framework for AI systems and establishes obligations for providers, deploy-ers, importers, distributors, and other stakeholders [1, 2]. The regulation aims to promote trustworthy AI and protect fundamen-tal rights; however, its complexity creates challenges for SMEs, startups, researchers, and other non-legal stakeholders seeking to understand their responsibilities and compliance obligations. Existing studies have identified several challenges for SMEs seeking to comply with the EU AI Act, including limited compliance resources, lack of regulatory expertise, uncertainty regarding risk classification and regula-tory interpretation, and the need to balance compliance activities with AI system development [3, 4, 5, 6]. These challenges create a gap between regu-latory requirements and practical implementation, highlighting the need for accessible governance support tools. This gap motivated the development of TESSA, a governance-oriented educational artifact designed to translate complex regulatory requirements into practical guidance. Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and generative AI have created new opportunities for educational, legal, and compliance sup-port applications. LLMs have increasingly served as the foundation for intel-ligent support systems across multiple domains. In legal and regulatory con-texts, systems such as Chatlaw have demonstrated the potential to provide advisory recommendations and improve access to specialized legal knowl-edge [7], and interactive legal assistants have been developed to support legal information access and non-specialists seeking legal guidance [8]. More broadly, AssistantGPT illustrates how LLM-based assistants can bridge the gap between complex system functionalities and intuitive user interaction [9], while GPTVoiceTasker represents an example of an LLM-based assistant 2designed to translate natural language requests into actionable task support [10]. However, the reliability of general-purpose LLMs remains a concern in domain-specific applications, motivating the use of domain-specific knowl-edge integration and grounding mechanisms to improve alignment, reduce hallucinations, and support trustworthy AI-assisted guidance. Despite growing research on the EU AI Act, AI governance, and LLM-based support systems, limited empirical evidence exists regarding governance-oriented educational artifacts designed to support SME compliance and AI governance learning. To address this gap, we have developed and evaluated TESSA, designed to support EU AI Act education and compliance guidance. The study addresses the following research questions (RQs): 

RQ1 To what extent can TESSA support SMEs and other non-legal stake-holders in understanding and applying EU AI Act requirements? and 

RQ2 How does governance-oriented grounding influence the governance align-ment, educational quality, and consistency of AI-generated compliance guidance compared with baseline ChatGPT? 

This study makes the following contributions: (i) a governance-oriented grounding approach for Custom GPT systems supporting AI governance education, (ii) TESSA, a design science research artifact for SME guidance under the EU AI Act, (iii) a multidimensional evaluation framework, and (iv) an empirical comparison with baseline ChatGPT. The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the research methodology, including the TESSA system architecture, governance-oriented grounding strategy, human-validated regulatory update mechanism, and multidimensional evaluation framework. Section 3 reports the evalua-tion results. Section 4 discusses the findings, implications, and limitations of the study. Finally, Section 5 concludes the paper and outlines future research directions. 

# 2 TESSA Design and Research Methodology 

2.1 Research Design 

This study followed the design science research methodology (DSRM) pro-posed by Peffers et al. [11] to design, develop, and evaluate the TESSA artifact. The research problem addressed the challenge faced by SMEs, start-ups, students, researchers, and other non-legal dedicated resources in understanding and operationalizing the requirements of the EU AI Act. Following the DSRM process, the study identified the practical problem of SMEs struggling with EU AI Act compliance, defined the objective of devel-oping TESSA, designed and developed the TESSA artifact within the Ope-nAI Custom GPT environment, demonstrated the artifact by applying it to 3representative EU AI Act compliance scenarios, and evaluated it through governance alignment assessment, human expert review, and consistency evaluation. This paper communicates the initial design and evaluation cy-cle, providing a foundation for future refinement. Following the DSR process, the resulting research artifact, TESSA, was designed, implemented, and evaluated to support SMEs and other non-legal stakeholders in interpreting EU AI Act requirements and translating them into practical governance-oriented actions. The evaluation investigated whether governance-oriented grounding improves the alignment, educational usefulness, and consistency of AI-assisted guidance. 

2.2 TESSA System Architecture 

TESSA was developed within OpenAI Custom GPT using the GPT-5 model available in January 2026. For comparison, the baseline system was the standard ChatGPT interface using the same underlying GPT-5 model with-out custom instructions, domain-specific knowledge sources, or governance-oriented grounding. TESSA supports users in interpreting EU AI Act re-quirements and translating them into practical governance-oriented actions. Figure 1 presents the deployed TESSA interface together with its overall system architecture 1. The evaluated TESSA configuration consisted of four main components: • System instructions • Published EU AI Act guidance knowledge base [1] • Use case AI List risk classification repository • Human-validated regulatory updates The architecture combines governance-oriented knowledge sources, a struc-tured grounding process, and governance-focused outputs. 

2.3 Governance-Oriented Grounding 

TESSA was designed using a governance-oriented grounding approach to support users in interpreting and applying the requirements of the EU AI Act. Rather than relying solely on general-purpose language model knowl-edge, the system combines governance-oriented instructions, domain-specific knowledge resources, risk classification support, and human-validated regu-latory updates. When responding to a query, TESSA identifies the relevant use case, stakeholder role, and potential AI risk classification before generating

2.4 Human-Validated Regulatory Update Mechanism 

As AI regulation and governance guidance continue to evolve, maintain-ing the currency of regulatory information represents a significant challenge for educational AI artifacts. To maintain current and validated regulatory knowledge, TESSA incorporates a human-validated regulatory update mech-anism designed to support the continuous monitoring of relevant regulatory developments while maintaining governance oversight. The update mechanism consists of two interconnected workflows. The first workflow focuses on automated information collection of candidate regulatory updates, including EU AI governance resources and academic databases, through an N8N-based automation workflow. Collected content is processed, merged, and filtered to remove duplicate entries before being stored in a structured review queue. The second workflow supports human review and controlled publication. Candidate updates are evaluated for relevance, accuracy, and consistency with the governance objectives of the system. Human reviewers assess whether the collected information should be approved or excluded from further use. Only validated updates are subse-5quently incorporated into TESSA through controlled update processes and associated repositories.

The evaluation of TESSA followed a multidimensional framework designed to assess governance alignment, educational suitability, reproducibility, and response consistency. The framework was developed to evaluate i) the cor-rectness of the responses and ii) their usefulness for SME-oriented compli-ance education and governance support. The evaluation consisted of four primary objectives (Obj): 1. To assess whether TESSA responses aligned with the published EU AI Act guidance knowledge base [1] and official EU AI Act sources [2]. 2. To evaluate the educational suitability and governance quality of the responses. 3. To compare TESSA with baseline ChatGPT using identical bench-mark scenarios. 4. To assess response consistency across repeated executions of the same scenarios. To address these objectives, a multidimensional evaluation framework comprising four complementary dimensions was adopted: traceability vali-dation, governance alignment evaluation, human expert review, and consis-tency evaluation. As shown in Figure 2 b) the framework consists of two evaluation phases. Phase A focuses on governance alignment through i) traceability validation, ii) governance alignment evaluation, and iii) human expert review. Phase B evaluates response consistency through repeated execution of benchmark scenarios. The evaluation dimensions were informed by design science research (DSR) evaluation principles [12], trustworthy AI concepts reflected in the NIST AI Risk Management Framework [13], and structured qualitative as-sessment approaches. Governance alignment, legal alignment, educational clarity, governance suitability, and hallucination risk were assessed using a three-level rating structure adapted from QualSyst [14]. 

2.5.1 Benchmark Design and Traceability Validation 

The benchmark scenarios were generated through a GPT-assisted process and subsequently reviewed and curated by the first author. The objec-tive was to create realistic educational, governance, and compliance-related questions that SMEs, startups, students, researchers, and other non-legal stakeholders might ask when seeking guidance on the EU AI Act. The benchmark was organized into ten categories: (i) AI literacy, (ii) startup and student projects, (iii) risk classification, (iv) compliance duties, (v) prohibited AI practices, (vi) high-risk AI, (vii) limited-risk AI, (viii) 7minimal-risk AI, (ix) GPAI and foundation models, and (x) SME perspec-tives, each containing ten questions, resulting in a total of 100 evaluation scenarios. Following scenario generation, responses were produced using TESSA and baseline ChatGPT. The responses were then evaluated against expected outcomes developed from a published EU AI Act guidance knowl-edge base [1] and relevant official EU AI Act sources [2]. This traceability validation supported Objective 1 by assessing the alignment between re-sponses and the grounding sources. 

2.5.2 Governance Alignment Evaluation 

A governance alignment evaluation was conducted to assess responses aligned with expected outcomes derived from a published EU AI Act guidance knowledge base [1] and official EU AI Act sources [2], and served as the benchmark for governance alignment assessment. This evaluation primarily addressed Obj. 1 and the supporting Obj. 3 by comparing TESSA and baseline ChatGPT under identical benchmark conditions. Responses from 100 benchmark scenarios were compared with the cor-responding expected outcome. The evaluation considered governance inter-pretation, risk classification, compliance recommendations, and consistency with the reference sources. Responses were assessed using the three-level alignment scale (Yes, Partial, No) presented in Table 1. The scoring ap-proach was adapted from the QualSyst framework [14]. The evaluation consisted of two stages. First, an LLM-assisted prelimi-nary review supported the initial classification of the responses. Second, the classifications were independently reviewed and validated by an experienced AI regulation lawyer practitioner with more than five years of professional legal experience in AI and data protection. The lawyer-reviewed assess-ments were used as the final governance alignment results reported in this study. The evaluation was intended to assess alignment with predefined ed-ucational and governance expectations rather than establish definitive legal interpretations. The resulting assessments enabled systematic comparison between TESSA and baseline ChatGPT and formed one component of the multidimensional evaluation framework.

2.5.4 Consistency Evaluation 

Consistency is an important quality attribute for evaluating design artifacts [12]. To assess reproducibility, each of the 100 benchmark scenarios was exe-cuted three times using both TESSA and baseline ChatGPT under identical prompting conditions, resulting a total of 600 responses. Five dimensions were evaluated: (i) risk classification consistency, ( ii) governance recommendation consistency, (iii) legal interpretation con-sistency, (iv) hallucination drift, and (v) reasoning variability. Responses were assessed using a three-level scale consisting of stable, minor variation, and major variation. Ratings were converted into numerical values (2–1–0) following adaptation of the QualSyst scoring logic [14] and aggregated into a normalized overall consistency score (OCS): 

OCS = total score obtained maximum possible score (1) Higher OCS values indicate greater consistency across repeated execu-tions. Detailed assessment criteria are presented in Table 1. Benchmark prompts, responses, reviewer assessments, and consistency records were doc-umented to support traceability and reproducibility. 

2.5.5 Data Collection and Analysis 

Data collected across both evaluation phases included response summaries, reviewer assessments, and consistency ratings, which were analyzed using quantitative and qualitative methods to compare TESSA and baseline Chat-GPT.

3.2 Human Expert Review Results 

The human expert review evaluated responses generated by TESSA and baseline ChatGPT using the framework described in Section 2.5.3. The final results reported in this section are based on the review conducted by an experienced AI regulation lawyer, while a separate AI regulation researcher review was used to support the assessment process. Table 3 summarizes the lawyer-reviewed results. Both TESSA and the baseline ChatGPT system demonstrated strong performance across the eval-uation dimensions. Educational clarity was the strongest category for both systems. TESSA received 99 "Strong" ratings and one "Acceptable" rating, while baseline ChatGPT received 92 "Strong" ratings and eight "Acceptable" ratings. No responses were classified as "Weak" in this category, indicating that both systems consistently produced explanations that were understand-able and accessible for the intended users. The review also identified differences in emphasis between the two sys-tems. Baseline ChatGPT received higher "Strong" ratings in Legal Align-ment and Overall Assessment, whereas TESSA received higher "Strong" rat-ings in Governance Suitability. Hallucination risk was generally low for both systems. TESSA received 61 low-, 36 medium-, and 3 high-risk ratings, while the baseline ChatGPT system received 78 low-, 21 medium-, and 1 high-risk ratings. The higher proportion of low risk ratings for baseline ChatGPT indicates fewer unsup-ported assumptions or over-generalized statements. Across both systems, 12 medium- and high-risk assessments primarily reflected unsupported assump-tions, incomplete explanations, or generalized statements rather than fabri-cated legal obligations or substantially incorrect regulatory interpretations. Similarly, the first author’s review found qualitative differences in em-phasis between the two systems. Overall, the human expert review indicates that the two systems exhibited different strengths rather than one consis-tently outperforming the other. Baseline ChatGPT received higher ratings in Legal Alignment, Hallucination Risk, and Overall Assessment, whereas TESSA received higher Governance Suitability ratings, reflecting its stronger emphasis on stakeholder responsibilities and practical compliance guidance. This suggests that governance-oriented grounding influenced the focus of TESSA responses toward stakeholder responsibilities, governance consid-erations, and compliance-oriented recommendations. 

3.3 Consistency Results

Both systems demonstrated a high degree of consistency across repeated executions. TESSA achieved an average OCS of 0.969, while baseline ChatGPT achieved an average OCS of 0.976. 3.4 Summary of Findings 

The evaluation demonstrated that both TESSA and baseline ChatGPT were capable of providing educational guidance related to the EU AI Act. Gov-ernance alignment was comparable between the two systems. TESSA re-ceived higher Governance Suitability ratings, while baseline ChatGPT re-ceived higher Legal Alignment and Overall Assessment ratings. Educational Clarity was a particular strength of both systems, indicating that EU AI Act concepts could be communicated effectively to the intended users. Both systems also demonstrated highly consistent performance across repeated executions, achieving comparable OCS values and low hallucina-tion drift. The findings suggest that governance-oriented grounding pro-vided structured governance guidance without reducing response consis-tency. Taken together, the results provide preliminary answers to the re-search questions. For RQ1, TESSA was able to translate complex EU AI Act requirements into understandable and actionable guidance. For RQ2, governance-oriented grounding contributed to structured governance-oriented educational guidance while maintaining consistency and low hallu-cination risk. 

# 4 Discussion 

4.1 From Regulatory Complexity to Actionable Guidance 

One objective of TESSA was to help SMEs and other non-legal stakehold-ers interpret EU AI Act requirements and translate them into practical governance actions. The evaluation results suggest that this objective was achieved. High ratings for Educational Clarity and Governance Suitability indicate that TESSA was able to provide understandable explanations while 14 supporting practical governance-oriented decision making. The governance-oriented grounding approach, which combines role identification, risk assess-ment, and domain-specific knowledge sources, appears to support the trans-formation of complex regulatory requirements into actionable guidance for resource-constrained stakeholders. 

4.2 Governance-Oriented Grounding Compared with Base-line ChatGPT 

The comparison with baseline ChatGPT revealed different strengths be-tween the two systems. The comparable legal performance observed in this study may reflect the increasing availability of EU AI Act knowledge in modern foundation models and other publicly available regulatory sources. Although governance alignment was comparable, between the two systems TESSA received higher Governance Suitability ratings, suggesting that governance-oriented grounding improved alignment with compliance-oriented educational objectives. In contrast, baseline ChatGPT often received higher Legal Alignment and Overall Assessment ratings, reflecting its ability to provide fluent and broadly applicable explanations. These findings suggest that the primary contribution of governance-oriented grounding is the structured presenta-tion of governance guidance rather than improving general legal knowledge. Baseline ChatGPT also performed well, suggesting that the primary con-tribution of TESSA lies in its structured governance guidance rather than exclusive access to regulatory knowledge. 

4.3 Implications for SMEs and Non-Legal Stakeholders 

The findings have practical implications for SMEs, students, researchers, and other non-legal stakeholders affected by the EU AI Act. TESSA may help bridge the gap between regulatory requirements and practical imple-mentation by providing structured explanations, risk-classification support, and stakeholder-specific guidance. Rather than replacing legal profession-als, systems such as TESSA can function as educational support tools that improve understanding of governance responsibilities, potential risks, and compliance considerations. 

4.4 Trustworthy AI and Human-Validated Regulatory Up-dates 

A key feature of TESSA is the incorporation of a human-validated regula-tory update mechanism. By combining automated monitoring of regulatory developments with human review, the mechanism supports the integration of updated information while maintaining human oversight. This approach 15 aligns with trustworthy AI principles and contributes to the reliability of governance-oriented educational guidance. 

4.5 Limitations 

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this study. First, TESSA and the baseline GPT were evaluated during the same evaluation period (April-June, 2026) using the latest and same underlying model version available at the time of testing. Future model updates may influence performance and affect comparative results. Second, the Custom GPT platform did not provide direct API access for automated benchmarking. Consequently, responses were collected and evaluated man-ually, increasing evaluation effort and introducing a potential risk of record-ing errors. Third, the evaluation involved human judgment when assessing governance alignment and response quality. Part of the human expert re-view was conducted by the first author, introducing a potential source of evaluator bias. To mitigate this risk, a structured evaluation framework, an LLM-assisted preliminary review, and independent legal validation were employed. Nevertheless, some degree of subjectivity remains unavoidable. Finally, the study evaluated a single governance-oriented Custom GPT con-figuration focused on EU AI Act education and compliance support. Addi-tional studies involving other regulatory frameworks, deployment contexts, and user populations would strengthen the generalizability of the findings. 

# 5 Conclusion and Future Work 

In this study, we developed and evaluated TESSA to support SMEs and other non-legal stakeholders in understanding and applying the EU AI Act. Using a multidimensional evaluation framework, we compared TESSA with baseline ChatGPT across 100 benchmark scenarios. The results indicated that TESSA provided strong governance-oriented guidance, high educational clarity, and stable performance across repeated executions. The findings suggest that governance-oriented grounding can improve the practical ap-plicability of AI-generated compliance guidance while maintaining response consistency. Future work will focus on extending TESSA beyond the EU AI Act by integrating additional AI regulations and governance frameworks into a unified knowledge environment and evaluating its use in real-world settings.
