## 摘要
COSIMA Cookbook 是一个开放教育资源，用于在 Jupyter notebooks 中分析海洋和海冰模式输出。它由澳大利亚海洋—海冰建模联盟（COSIMA）开发，作为面向研究人员、学生和从业者的社区资源，服务于处理大型网格化数据集的工作，尤其是 ACCESS 海洋—海冰模式配置的输出。Cookbook 以美食学为主题进行组织：“Cooking Tutorials”讲授处理模式输出的通用、可迁移技术，而“Easy Recipes”“Advanced Recipes”和“Regional Specialties”则是自包含的 notebook，展示如何开展具体的诊断与分析。在此过程中，这些 notebook 演示了用于大规模数据科学分析的开源工具，包括 Intake catalogues、Xarray、Dask、xgcm、xESMF、eofs、Parcels、matplotlib 和 Cartopy。Cookbook 应对了分析气候模式输出时高昂的入门成本：它将专家的、往往是隐性的知识，打包成完整、文档完善且可复用的工作流，并采用研究人员实际工作所使用的媒介。它在 GitHub 上公开开发，作为文档网站发布，并通过 pull request 和定期 hackathon 由社区持续审查和扩展。它所提供的对数据和分析的便捷访问，促进了 ACCESS 海洋和海冰模式配置的国际采用，并已助力远超 100 篇同行评审论文和 20 多个博士项目。

## Abstract
The COSIMA Cookbook is an open educational resource for analysing ocean and sea-ice model output in Jupyter notebooks. It has been developed by the Consortium for Ocean--Sea Ice Modelling in Australia (COSIMA) as a community resource for researchers, students, and practitioners working with large gridded datasets, especially output from the ACCESS ocean--sea ice model configurations. The Cookbook is organised around a gastronomy theme: ``Cooking Tutorials'' teach generic, transferable techniques for handling model output, while "Easy Recipes", "Advanced Recipes" and "Regional Specialties" are self-contained notebooks that show how to perform concrete diagnostics and analyses. Along the way, the notebooks demonstrate open-source tools for the scientific analysis of large data, including Intake catalogues, Xarray, Dask, xgcm, xESMF, eofs, Parcels, matplotlib and Cartopy. The Cookbook addresses the steep entry cost of analysing climate model output: it packages expert, often tacit, knowledge into complete, well-documented and reusable workflows, in the same medium in which researchers actually work. It is developed openly on GitHub, is published as a documentation website, and is continuously reviewed and extended by its community through pull requests and regular hackathons. The easy access to data and analysis it provides has contributed to the international adoption of ACCESS ocean and sea-ice model configurations, and has facilitated well over 100 peer-reviewed papers and more than 20 PhD projects.
