Integrating Generative Artificial Intelligence with Learning Style Models: Opportunities, Challenges, and Future Directions 

Malithi Nawarathne *, Prasanna Haddela, Nuwan Kodagoda 

Faculty of Computing, Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology, Sri Lanka 

Abstract - Generative Artificial Intelligence (Generative AI) has emerged as a powerful tool for transforming learning. This review examines how Generative AI can be integrated with established learning style models (VARK, Kolb, Felder-Silverman, and Honey & Mumford) to support student engagement in large online classes. Following PRISMA 2020 guidelines, we searched Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar (2015–2026) and included 41 studies that met the eligibility criteria. The review maps Generative AI capabilities to learning style preferences, synthesizes existing implementations, highlights benefits and challenges, and proposes pathways for future research and practice. Despite ongoing debates regarding the empirical validity of learning styles, Generative AI enables flexible, multimodal, and real-time adaptation. This study offers practical insights for educators and developers seeking to build more effective, learner centered learning systems. 

Keywords – Generative AI, Learning styles, Personalized learning, Student engagement. 

1. Introduction 

The recent emergence of Generative Artificial Intelligence (Generative AI) has unlocked powerful capabilities that are transforming education (Baidoo-Anu & Ansah, 2023; Murtaza et al., 2024; Pesovski et al., 2024). Generative AI now supports intelligent course design, real-time assessment, tutoring, interactive content generation, and adaptive learning experiences (Bender, 2024; Biswas, 2023; Chan & Lee, 2023; Pesovski et al., 2024; Sharma & Yadav, 2022; Yu & Guo, 2023). In particular, Generative AI excels at delivering immediate, scalable, and personalized learning by dynamically generating tailored content, feedback, and learning pathways based on individual learner data (Abdullah et al., 2024; Chiu, 2023; Hashmi et al., 2024; Oseremi Onesi-Ozigagun et al., 2024; Randver, 2025). 

Traditional teaching methods, however, continue to deliver instruction to all students at the same pace and in the same format. This one-size-fits-all approach struggles to accommodate individual differences in learning styles, preferences, prior knowledge, and needs. Personalized learning addresses this limitation by tailoring educational experiences to each learner’s unique profile, thereby enhancing motivation, engagement, efficiency, and overall satisfaction (Ambele et al., 2022; Asthana et al., 2023; Grant & Basye, 2014; Keefe, 2007; Shemshack & Spector, 2020). The integration of Generative AI further strengthens personalization by enabling immediate, context-aware feedback and adaptive content delivery (Chiu, 2023; Oseremi Onesi-Ozigagun et al., 2024). 

Despite growing interest, researchers have highlighted that substantial work remains to fully realize personalized learning at scale (Asthana et al., 2023; Chiu, 2023). Established learning style models such as VARK, Kolb, Felder-Silverman, and Honey & Mumford offer practical frameworks for understanding learner differences and guiding personalization efforts. 

Nevertheless, despite rapid Generative AI adoption in education, no comprehensive synthesis currently exists that systematically links Generative AI capabilities with these established learning style frameworks for large-scale online personalization. This review addresses that critical gap by examining how Generative AI can be effectively integrated with learning style models to support scalable, learner-centered education. We now turn to why personalized delivery via learning styles is both necessary and underexplored, where this review was guided by the following research questions: 1. How can Generative AI capabilities be mapped to established learning style models (VARK, Kolb, Felder-Silverman, and Honey & Mumford)? 

2. What implementations of Generative AI integrated with learning style models currently exist? 

3. What are the benefits, challenges, and limitations of this integration? 

4. What future directions should be pursued for research and practice? 

This study followed a systematic review approach to identify and analyze research at the intersection of Generative AI and learning styles for application in large online classes. We searched major databases, including Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Google Scholar, using combinations of keywords of “Generative AI”, “learning styles”, “virtual assistants”, “teaching assistants”, and “personalized learning”. Hence, studies were included if they met the following requirements, 

1. Articles that were peer-reviewed journal articles, or conference papers published in English. 

2. Articles focused on the application or integration of Generative with at least one established learning style model (VARK, Kolb, Felder-Silverman, Honey & Mumford). 

3. Articles that addressed personalization, adaptive learning, teaching assistants, and virtual assistants in educational contexts. 

4. Articles that were published between 1 st of January 2015, and 31 st of March 2026. Figure 1: Prisma diagram related to the study 

2. Learning Styles 

Learning styles are preferred methods for processing information (Alzain et al., 2018; Khanal et al., 2014). Aligning teaching with styles improves outcomes, engagement, and efficiency (Abdullah et al., 2024; Budiyanto et al., 2017; Mahmoud Hawa & Tılfarlıoğlu, 2019). Further, aligning teaching with preferred styles moves beyond “one-size-fits-all” approaches, enhancing efficiency, problem-solving, and long-term achievement (Abdullah et al., 2024; Budiyanto et al., 2017). It addresses individual differences, boosts motivation, and is crucial as class sizes grow and technology evolves (Alhathli et al., 2017; Mahmoud Hawa & Tılfarlıoğlu, 2019). 

However, although the concept of learning styles has faced significant criticism for lacking robust empirical evidence (Newton & Salvi, 2020; Pashler et al., 2008), it continues to inform practical research in adaptive and AI-driven education (Castro et al., 2025; Nunez et al., 2026; Randver, 2025; Ren et al., 2026, p. 2026; Troussas et al., 2025). Recent studies argue that, despite theoretical debates, learning styles frameworks enable dynamic personalization in technology-mediated environments, where Generative AI can generate multimodal content to accommodate preferences without rigid matching (Chandrasekera et al., 2024; Cohen, 2023; Fernando & Premadasa, 2024; Nunez et al., 2026; Oseremi Onesi-Ozigagun et al., 2024; Ren et al., 2026). Rather than framing Generative AI's objective as matching a predefined style, we emphasize its capacity for multimodal flexibility. By offering the same content in multiple formats at once, Generative AI allows students to select what works best for their particular context, avoiding the rigidity of forcing all learners into a single stylistic mode. 

Therefore, this review was conducted with the aim of cautioning against over-reliance on styles as fixed traits while exploring Generative AI's potential to leverage them for flexible, engaging adaptation in large online classes. 3. Teaching Virtual Assistants from Past to Present 

Virtual assistants have functioned in organizing the study process, managing time, increasing motivation, and engagement in the study process (Gubareva & Lopes, 2020), (Pereira et al., 2022). These tools with the usage of AI, can provide personal assistance to students in the form of feedback, suggestions, scaffolding, and information retrieval. Before the introduction of Generative AI, many virtual teaching assistants were created with the use of AI based tools and technologies (Benedetto et al., 2019; Escotet, 2023; Essel et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2024; Mekni et al., 2020). However, the learning process can greatly benefit from Generative AI's integration of real-time feedback, adaptive material distribution, and powerful analytics. 

Hence, studies on personalization and adaptive learning highlight Generative AI's role in tailored content, feedback, and engagement (Chan & Hu, 2023; Chen et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024; Michel-Villarreal et al., 2023; Mittal et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024). While these studies offer important findings and information, they also have several limitations in implementing personalized learning solutions via learning styles that can adapt according to changing student preferences. In order to provide personalized, flexible solutions, this combination pinpoints the necessity for research that combines Generative AI with pedagogical approaches. These systems can adapt to the demands of students in real time feedback. Summarizing these studies highlights both the progress made and the gaps that remain, particularly in how Generative AI can extend or improve upon earlier approaches. Considering the existing work that has been done, it is evident that Felder-Silverman and VARK models have been used widely to propose solutions with the aid of AI related technologies. Kolb’s model has been used mainly with the experiential learning concept in research works. Honey and Mumford model, however, has been used at a rare level. 

Further, the evidence in tables 2, 3, 4, and 5 illustrates that while AI and ML techniques such as clustering, predictive modeling, and adaptive systems have successfully incorporated learning style models, FSLSM, VARK, Kolb, and Honey and Mumford, these implementations are often limited to static matching of learners to predefined content. Generative AI, by contrast, allows real-time adaptation, multimodal output, and conversational interaction, which can overcome the rigidity of earlier systems. Thus, the integration of Generative AI with these models represents a natural evolution beyond past personalization strategies.

> 4.2. Enhancing the learning experience in large classes through Generative AI personalization

However, while learning style-based personalization offers significant pedagogical value, it addresses only part of the broader personalization challenge. To realize the full potential of Generative AI, it is critical to extend the scope beyond predefined typologies and toward a more holistic, context-sensitive view of learner diversity. While learning styles serve as an important first layer in personalizing education, modern learners exhibit more complex, evolving needs that go beyond sensory and cognitive preferences. Personalization in education must also accommodate factors such as student engagement, motivation, student isolation, providing student feedback, student-teacher interaction, and diverse knowledge levels. Hence, beyond aligning content with individual learning styles, Generative AI offers unique affordances for enriching the broader learning experience. This section explores several pedagogical dimensions, ranging from emotional engagement to individualized feedback, through which Generative AI can deepen personalization in education.  

> 4.2.1. Student engagement and motivation

Engagement and motivation are often compromised in online learning due to passive instruction and a lack of personalization. Learning styles, when supported by Generative AI, can reignite engagement by presenting content in formats students naturally respond to. Generative AI enhances this by dynamically adapting the format and interactivity level, using reinforcement learning, feedback signals from learners’ inputs to keep them motivated and involved (Baidoo-Anu & Ansah, 2023; Chan & Lee, 2023).  

> 4.2.2. Emotional state and cognitive load

Online learners often experience emotional fatigue, confusion, or anxiety, particularly when instruction does not align with their preferences. Generative AI can monitor sentiment and adapt instruction not only to how learners prefer to learn, but also to how they're feeling while learning. AI generated real world simulations and storytelling tasks can build emotional resonance. LLMs can adjust tone, simplify content complexity, and provide emotionally reassuring feedback based on observed learner frustration (Baidoo-Anu & Ansah, 2023; Boussaha & Drissi, 2021). Through natural language understanding, Generative AI can act as a responsive tutor, identifying confusion or stress signals and adapting content or pacing accordingly.  

> 4.2.3. Reducing student isolation and strengthening interaction

Large online classrooms often lead to student anonymity and a sense of disconnection. Generative AI, integrated with learning style insights, can foster personalized interaction and reduce isolation. Generative AI tools can also simulate group work experiences, design peer-mirroring, or recommend tailored discussion prompts to foster community for learners who might otherwise remain disengaged (Hashmi et al., 2024). Generative AI tools tailored using learning style inputs, can serve as simulated conversational partners (Baidoo-Anu & Ansah, 2023; Hashmi et al., 2024; Pesovski et al., 2024). This human like interaction restores some of the relational elements missing from traditional online learning environments, thereby increasing perceived support and reducing social isolation. 4.2.4. Personalized feedback providing 

Feedback is critical to learning, yet in large-scale virtual settings, it is often delayed or generic. Generative AI tools can deliver immediate, personalized feedback that aligns with a student's preferred mode of processing (Chan & Lee, 2023, 2023; Michel-Villarreal et al., 2023). It can tailor its explanations to match student preferences. Tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, have already proved to assist students track their goals and generate personalized visual learning analytics dashboards tailored to their learning style (Randver, 2025). Such alignment ensures feedback is not only timely but also cognitively resonant and reinforces student growth more effectively.  

> 4.2.5. Supporting diverse knowledge levels

In large classrooms, the spread of prior knowledge and competencies is wide. Generative AI, informed by learning style models, can dynamically adjust task difficulty and presentation. Through diagnostic quizzes or AI driven knowledge graphs, students can be categorized not only by level but also by preferred learning mode, enabling tailored learning paths that scale effectively across hundreds or thousands of students (Chan & Lee, 2023; Michel-Villarreal et al., 2023; Mittal et al., 2024; Pesovski et al., 2024).  

> 4.2.6. Enhancing student-teacher interaction

While Generative AI is often used by students, it can also personalize support for teachers, using learning style data to help instructors monitor which students are disengaged or struggling and how best to intervene (Chan & Lee, 2023). Teachers could receive Generative AI generated recommendations on grouping students with complementary learning styles for collaborative projects. Analytics dashboards can highlight mismatches between student preferences and performance, prompting pedagogical adjustments. This allows educators to maintain apersonalized presence at scale, closing the feedback loop and enhancing the human element of online teaching. 

5. Conclusion 

Considering the propositions discussed in this section, it is evident that introducing personalized learning with the integration of Generative AI tools is a plausible solution that can assist students to learn contentedly. The integration of learning styles with Generative AI has shown great potential in transforming the education field. Generative AI can overcome the limitations introduced in existing traditional and online learning techniques as discussed in this study. Most importantly, the Generative AI’s ability to adapt to present circumstances and provide fast responses can allow the students and educators carry out their tasks efficiently. While this study highlights the potential of Generative AI in supporting personalized learning through established learning style frameworks, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations and ethical considerations. Learning style theories have been a topic that has received attention due to critics arguing about oversimplification. Ethical considerations should also be taken into account in the usage of Generative AI related tools, as the research might involve working with sensitive data. Further research should therefore be conducted while addressing the ethical considerations that would result in virtual learning environments, balancing automation with pedagogical intents. In conclusion, the combination of learning styles with Generative AI represents a huge step forward in personalized education. The path ahead is not without its challenges, but the potential will reward empowered learners and educators to perform their duties efficiently. This review hence lays a solid roadmap that can be considered for the development of systems that can adopt Generative AI and learning styles. Conflicts of interest declaration
