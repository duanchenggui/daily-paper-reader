## 摘要
儿童图书插图承载着发展、教学和文化方面的责任，这些责任通常在指南中被正式规定。随着 AI 系统开始进入儿童出版流程，既作为评估者又作为生成者，一个自然的问题随之而来：它们能否可靠地满足所需的结构、安全和文化标准？我们围绕三项标准对此展开研究：指南合规性、安全性和文化对齐，并使用印度尼西亚的《图书分级指南》作为政府发布的评估框架，该框架针对四个阅读水平给出了明确的视觉标准。为了解视觉语言模型（VLM）能在多大程度上识别并应用这些指南标准，我们首先在真实出版的儿童图书页面上对它们进行评估，并将其评估结果与人类基线进行比较。随后，为考察 AI 生成的插图是否达到相同标准，我们将 VLM 评判器应用于两个生成模型产出的图像，并与一部分人类插画师评分者的结果进行交叉核验。我们发现，VLM 在合规性和安全性等明确标准上较为可靠，但在文化对齐方面存在不足，会系统性地漏掉人类评分者标记为文化上不适当的内容。AI 生成的插图也表现出同样的盲点：它们满足结构性和安全性标准，却未能捕捉到人类评分者一致识别的隐含文化细节。

## Abstract
Children's book illustrations carry developmental, pedagogical, and cultural responsibilities that are often formally codified in guidelines. As AI systems begin entering children's publishing pipelines, both as evaluators and as generators, a natural question arises: can they reliably meet the structural, safety, and cultural standards required? We investigate this across three criteria: guideline compliance, safety, and cultural alignment, using Indonesia's Book Leveling Guideline as a government-issued evaluation framework with explicit visual criteria for four reading levels. To understand how well VLMs can identify and apply these guideline criteria, we first evaluate them on real published children's book pages and compare their assessments against a human baseline. To then examine whether AI-generated illustrations meet the same standards, we apply the VLM judge to images produced by two generation models and cross-check them against a subset of human illustrator raters. We find that VLMs are reliable on explicit criteria such as compliance and safety, but fall short on cultural alignment, systematically missing content that human raters flag as culturally inappropriate. AI-generated illustrations show the same blind spot: they satisfy structural and safety criteria but fail to capture implicit cultural details that human raters consistently identify.
